Purdie Shuffle

A half-time shuffle with ghost notes — slow it down and keep the ghosts soft.

Classic Breakbeats

The two most-sampled drum breaks in history. These are the core one-bar grooves transcribed onto the 16th-note grid — the original recordings drift in timing and vary bar to bar, so treat these as the playable skeleton and add your own feel.

The Amen Break — Gregory Coleman (The Winstons, "Amen, Brother")

Backbeats on 2 and 4 with ghost notes, kick on 1 and the "&", and the driving kicks into beat 3. Authentic tempo is brisk (~136) — slow it down to drill the ghosts.

Funky Drummer — Clyde Stubblefield (James Brown)

The single-handed 16th-note hi-hat pattern is the challenge here, with two open hi-hats (the o/+ open-then-close), ghost notes peppered around the backbeats, and the tricky ghost on the "a" of 3 right before beat 4.
